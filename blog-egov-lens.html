eGov-Lens: Multi-Dimensional Aspect-Based Feedback Analysis for Bengali e-Government

How we curated a 34,001-comment Bengali public service feedback dataset and benchmarked transformer architectures with Entropy-weighted TOPSIS for deployment-ready governance intelligence.

In modern public administration, citizen feedback across digital touchpoints represents a vital pulse on public service delivery. However, citizen feedback in Bengali is frequently unstructured, colloquial, noisy, and covers multiple operational dimensions simultaneously.

To address this, we developed eGov-Lens—a comprehensive, multi-dimensional aspect-based NLP framework for analyzing citizen feedback across 12 major Bangladeshi e-government services.

Curating the 34,001-Comment Dataset

We curated and manually annotated a corpus of 34,001 Bengali comments collected between 2020 and 2024 from the official public forums and channels of 12 critical e-government services:

12 Covered E-Government Service Sectors:

• BRTA (Road Transport) • BDRIS (Birth & Death Reg.) • BTRC (Telecom Reg.) • Consumer Rights • Agriculture (DAE) • Digital Bangladesh • Education Portals • Election Commission • Medical & Health • Police Services • Postal Services • Teletalk Services

Multi-Aspect Analytical Framework

Instead of generic sentiment polarity, eGov-Lens decomposes each citizen review into four policy-actionable aspect categories:

  • Sentiment: Overall public sentiment polarity (Positive, Negative, Neutral).
  • Ease of Finding (EoF): Navigation and accessibility of specific service workflows.
  • Responsiveness: Turnaround speed, server downtime, and administrative responsiveness.
  • Accuracy: Information correctness, document precision, and transactional integrity.

Model Benchmarking & Architecture

We benchmarked 7 distinct machine learning and transformer representation combinations:

  • Text Representations: CountVectorizer (BoW n-grams), Multilingual BERT (mBERT), and pretrained domain-specific BanglaBERT.
  • Classifiers: Support Vector Classifier (SVC), Logistic Regression (LR), and Multinomial Naive Bayes (MNB).

Deployment-Aware Entropy-Weighted TOPSIS

In resource-constrained governmental infrastructure, raw accuracy alone is insufficient. We utilized the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) with objective Shannon Entropy weighting across 6 critical operational criteria:

TOPSIS Closeness Coefficient Formula:

$$C_i^* = \frac{D_i^-}{D_i^+ + D_i^-}, \quad C_i^* \in [0, 1]$$

where $D_i^+$ and $D_i^-$ represent the Euclidean distance to the positive-ideal and negative-ideal solutions across Accuracy, F1-score, Recall, ROC-AUC, Training Time, and Inference Latency.
