PREFACE

Engineering design is a creative and systematic process of developing a product or system to meet specific

needs. It is an iterative process involving repeated problem-solving, testing, and refinement cycles. This

report presents the design of a semi-automated shoe-cleaning machine. The machine can clean, and polish,

making it a versatile tool for personal and commercial use.

The report begins by discussing the need for the machine and the target market. It then describes the design

process, including the problem definition, concept generation, and evaluation. The report also discusses

the manufacturing process and cost analysis. The goal of this report is to share the design process for the

semi-automated shoe-cleaning machine and to encourage students to engage in creative product design.

The need for a semi-automated shoe-cleaning machine is evident in households and commercial spaces

such as hotels and offices. Users need a machine that can perform a variety of tasks, such as cleaning,

polishing, and sanitizing shoes efficiently. Current methods on the market are either too labor-intensive or

lack versatility. The target market for the semi-automated shoe-cleaning machine is individuals and

organizations that value convenience and efficiency in shoe care. The machine is user-friendly and can be

utilized by people with varying levels of technical expertise.

The design process for the semi-automated shoe-cleaning machine began with a problem definition. We

identified the needs of users and the shortcomings of current solutions available on the market. We then

generated a variety of concepts for the machine. These concepts were evaluated based on their

functionality, manufacturability, and cost. The final design of the machine was chosen for its ease of use,

versatility, and cost-effectiveness. The machine is made of durable materials, ensuring longevity and easy

maintenance. It is also equipped with safety features to protect the user during operation.

We hope that this report will be informative and helpful to students who are interested in product design.

We also hope that this report will inspire students to engage in creative and innovative product

development.

ABSTRACT

The "Semi-Automated Shoe Cleaning Machine" report presents a comprehensive study conducted in the

context of Bangladesh, focusing on the development and evaluation of an innovative cleaning solution

that combines traditional cleaning methods with semi-automated technology. In a country where efficient

and hygienic cleaning solutions are becoming increasingly necessary, this product is designed to cater to

individuals and small businesses seeking an affordable and user-friendly shoe cleaning solution.

Based on the market survey results, we determined the customer requirements and technical specifications,

along with their relationships and correlations. We then conducted qualitative analysis of material,

manufacturing processes, and joining process selections. Following this, we performed quantitative

analyses by calculating relative emphasis coefficients using the Digital Logic Method and performance

indices. Additionally, we conducted equivalent (von Mises) stress, total deformation, fatigue life, and

fatigue safety factor analyses using Ansys. Finally, we performed cost, breakeven, and sensitivity analyses

for our product.

Hopefully, this report will encourage students to design and analyze their own innovative products.

Introduction

1.1Product design

Product design is a process of generating new products or services or upgradation of existing products

through a set of strategic and tactical activities focusing on customer needs and market demand. The

design usually has to satisfy certain goals and constraints, may take into account aesthetic, functional,

economic, or socio-political consideration, and is expected to interact with a certain environment.

The most common factors that are taken into consideration in an effective product design are- ergonomics,

usability, and manufacturing feasibility. In order to improve the quality and life span of the product, it

goes through an analytical process and relies on a problem-solving approach. It is about problem-solving,

visualizing the needs of the user, and bringing a solution. Design concepts that can be engineered are

special. We are not trying just to design something that looks new. We are trying to find a balance between

all the different factors that influence a product.

    Incorporating the Voice of Customer in Product Design with Quality Function Deployment (QFD)

    3.1Introduction

    According to the Japanese, Quality Function Deployment (QFD) is "Listen to the voice of the market (customers)," which refers to understanding the design problem to create a high-quality product. One must comprehend the design problem thoroughly. The process of turning user requests and requirements into a technical description of what has to be created is known as Quality Function Deployment, or QFD. The goal of QFD is to create a product that meets the customer's exact needs, rather than focusing on the builder's existing skills or knowledge.

    In QFD, multi-skilled teams work together to understand customer needs and define technical requirements for each stage. The key benefits of QFD include a strong focus on customer needs, ensuring that products are developed based on what the customer values rather than depending solely on technical expertise. QFD promotes teamwork by bringing together multi-skilled teams, which enhances a deeper understanding of the project requirements. This approach also improves production efficiency and helps reduce development time and costs. The QFD process emphasizes listening to customers, refining specifications, and assessing how well both the company and competitors meet customer expectations.

    Functional Decomposition

    4.1Introduction

    Functional decomposition is an analytical approach where we break down a complex system into manageable component functions and processes. This technique is crucial for developing and managing intricate products, as it allows a top-down view that displays increasingly detailed levels of functionality within a system. A functional decomposition model or diagram visualizes the total breakdown and structure of a product's processes, helping designers understand how each part contributes to the overall purpose.

    The decomposition process involves four essential steps:

    01

    Identify the Primary Function

    The design's core function must be distilled into a simple statement and represented in a "black box," where energy, material, and information inputs and outputs define the boundaries of the system.

    02

    Develop Sub-functions

    The primary function is broken down into specific sub-functions necessary to support the system's purpose, ensuring each component contributes effectively to the main goal.

    03

    Organize Sub-functions

    By arranging the sub-functions in a logical order, the process flow becomes streamlined, which is particularly important for complex products with multiple interacting elements.

    04

    Refine Sub-functions

    Each sub-function is examined for further decomposition. This ensures that every aspect of the function is addressed, contributing to the integrity of the overall system.

    4.2Black Box Model of Functional Decomposition

    The black box model is a simplified approach to functional decomposition, where the system is represented abstractly with its inputs and outputs for energy, material, and information. This model provides a high-level overview of the functionality without delving into internal details. It is especially useful for understanding or describing complex systems in terms of what they do rather than how they work, enabling a clear representation of input-output relationships.

    Future Scope & Limitations

    9.1Introduction

    This chapter examines the future scope and limitations of the Semi-Automated Shoe Cleaning Machine, a device designed to simplify and improve the process of cleaning footwear in residential and commercial settings. With a focus on semi-automation, ease of use, and affordability, the product aims to address the challenges of maintaining shoe hygiene in busy urban and rural households. This section explores how the design could evolve to include advanced features such as full automation, enhanced cleaning efficiency, and smart monitoring systems to meet growing consumer demands. Furthermore, the chapter identifies and evaluates key limitations, such as reliance on external power sources, limited cleaning versatility, and maintenance concerns. By outlining potential improvements and acknowledging existing constraints, this chapter provides a strategic roadmap for the product's continued development and market relevance.

    9.2Future Scope

    The Semi-Automated Shoe Cleaning Machine has significant potential for future enhancements to increase its utility and customer appeal. Some key areas for engineering and functional development include:

    01 Automation
    Fully Automated Cleaning Cycle

    02 Intelligence

    03 Versatility

    06 Materials

    9.3Limitations

    While the Semi-Automated Shoe Cleaning Machine offers a practical solution to footwear maintenance, certain technical and operational limitations currently constrain its performance and commercial versatility. The identified operational constraints include:

    L1 Power Supply
    Grid Electricity Dependency

    The machine currently relies strictly on 220V AC wall power, which restricts its operational flexibility in rural, mobile, or power-interrupted localities without auxiliary generation.

    L2 Geometry
    Tread Inaccessibility on Deep Grooves

    Standard cylindrical and side brushes exhibit limited penetration when scrubbing complex, deeply lugged hiking boot soles or heavily textured upper embellishments.

    L3 Chassis Size
    Footwear Dimensional Constraints

    The fixed-dimension washing bay cannot accommodate non-standard footwear sizes (e.g. extreme oversized work boots or very small infant shoes) without risk of poor brush contact.

    L4 Fluid Volume
    Limited Fluid Reservoir Capacity

    The onboard water and liquid detergent tanks require frequent manual replenishment during peak high-volume operational cycles in commercial lobbies.

    L5 Maintenance
    Mechanical Wear on Brushes & Rollers

    Continuous abrasive friction against diverse rubber soles precipitates periodic bristle bending and polymer degradation, dictating regular replacement intervals.

    L6 Automation
    User Intervention in Semi-Automation

    The semi-automated architecture necessitates manual shoe insertion, orientation holding, and cycle triggering, falling short of a fully autonomous hands-off appliance.

    L7 Acoustics
    Acoustic Noise During Peak Operation

    Mechanical gearing, spur gears, and motor rotation generate audible operating noise that may cause minor disruption in quiet office environments or executive boardrooms.

    L8 Effluent Mgmt
    Wastewater & Residue Filtration

    The existing gravity-drain system lacks integrated centrifugal or multi-stage filtration to separate heavy mud cakes and particulate sludge from the effluent liquid.

    The Semi-Automated Shoe Cleaning Machine presents an innovative and accessible solution to maintaining shoe hygiene, particularly in environments where time and convenience are critical. By integrating semi-automation, compact design, and essential cleaning features, it successfully addresses key pain points for everyday users.
