                               Functional Decomposition
4.1 Introduction
Functional decomposition is an analytical approach where we break down a complex system
into manageable component functions and processes. This technique is crucial for developing
and managing intricate products, as it allows a top-down view that displays increasingly
detailed levels of functionality within a system. A functional decomposition model or diagram
visualizes the total breakdown and structure of a product’s processes, helping designers
understand how each part contributes to the overall purpose.
The decomposition process involves four essential steps:
Step 1: Identify the Primary Function
The design’s core function must be distilled into a simple statement and represented in a “black
box,” where energy, material, and information inputs and outputs define the boundaries of the
system.
Step 2: Develop Sub-functions
The primary function is broken down into specific sub-functions necessary to support the
system's purpose, ensuring each component contributes effectively to the main goal.
Step 3: Organize Sub-functions
By arranging the sub-functions in a logical order, the process flow becomes streamlined, which
is particularly important for complex products with multiple interacting elements.
Step 4: Refine Sub-functions
Each sub-function is examined for further decomposition. This ensures that every aspect of the
function is addressed, contributing to the integrity of the overall system.


4.2 Black Box Model of Functional Decomposition
The black box model is a simplified approach to functional decomposition, where the system
is represented abstractly with its inputs and outputs for energy, material, and information. This
model provides a high-level overview of the functionality without delving into internal details.
It is especially useful for understanding or describing complex systems in terms of what they
do rather than how they work, enabling a clear representation of input-output relationships.

4.3 Component Hierarchy
Component hierarchy is a very effective method of listing the components required to design
a product. The component hierarchy methodology simply distinguishes between the core
portions of the product and then lists the components for those portions. The portions are simply
termed as sub-assembly. When the function of all sub-assemblies are satisfied, the prime
function is satisfied. This can be repeated iteratively down several levels developing a function
tree. Function trees are fast and easy to construct, but this ease of construction comes at the
expense of understanding interactions between sub- assemblies

4.5 Conclusion
In conclusion, the use of the black box model, component hierarchy, and cluster function
structure provides a comprehensive approach to the functional decomposition of the semi-
automated shoe cleaning machine. The black box model offers a concise overview of the
machine’s inputs and outputs, while the component hierarchy breaks down each part’s role in
the overall system. The cluster function structure further illustrates the flow of material and
energy, providing a deeper understanding of each component's contribution. Collectively, these
decomposition methods contribute to a well-structured and efficient design, making the semi-
automated shoe cleaning machine both functional and user-friendly.

5.1 Introduction
The systematic process of designing, which includes tasks such as gathering information,
planning, and effective communication, is often referred to as design analysis. Product
development is typically divided into several stages, each involving specific activities. These
stages frequently require different analyses, such as market research to understand consumer
behavior or financial evaluations to identify risks and investment opportunities. In the case of
the "Automated Shoe Cleaner," certain design evaluations are essential to transform the concept
into a tangible product.
A critical phase in product development is design analysis, which helps minimize or eliminate
reliance on extensive physical prototyping and testing. Through detailed research into customer
needs, material selection, manufacturing methods, and assembly processes, a prototype for the
"Automated Shoe Cleaner" has been developed. This section will provide an overview of the
"Automated Shoe Cleaner" components skillfully designed using SolidWorks software.
Additionally, a detailed stress analysis, conducted using Ansys software, will be presented to
ensure the product’s design is both robust and reliable.
